B. My Research

I.

Environmental Pharmacoepidemiology

I study how environmental stressors, including extreme heat, wildfire smoke, and air pollution, affect clinical outcomes among patients with chronic cardiometabolic conditions. A major focus of my work is understanding whether medications or medication burden may modify patients' vulnerability to environmental-related risks.

Related Publications ▾
Case-Crossover Analysis of Short-Term Particulate Matter and Health Outcomes in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation — JAHA, 2026 In press ▾

Background: Residents of western U.S. states face elevated fine particulate matter (PM2.5) exposure from increasingly frequent and intense wildfires. Whether short-term PM2.5 exposure increases the odds of hospitalization or emergency care in patients with established atrial fibrillation (AF) remains unclear.

Methods: Using 2021 Medicare data, we identified patients with established AF in western U.S. states. Zip code–level daily PM2.5 was estimated from a 1 km² geospatial model; high PM2.5 was defined as ≥9.0 µg/m³. Odds ratios for all-cause and cardiovascular (CVD) hospitalization and ER visits were estimated with conditional logistic regression in a case-crossover design, adjusting for extreme heat and public holidays.

Results: Among 26,158 patients (mean age 79 ± 9.0 years), high PM2.5 was associated with 10% higher odds of CVD hospitalization (95% CI 1%–20%), 24% higher odds of AF-related hospitalization (95% CI 3%–48%), and 34% higher odds of AF-related ER visits (95% CI 6%–70%). No significant interaction was observed between PM2.5 and extreme heat.

Conclusion: On days with elevated PM2.5, patients with AF had higher odds of AF-related and cardiovascular hospitalizations and ER visits, highlighting the need for targeted strategies to protect vulnerable populations from air pollution.

Risk of Hospitalization in Patients with Heart Failure Exposed to Extreme Heat and Poor Air Quality in the Western United States — Circulation, 2025 (AHA Scientific Sessions) DOI → ▾

Introduction: Patients with cardiovascular disease are vulnerable to extreme weather, but limited data exist for heart failure (HF). In the western U.S., where heat waves and wildfire exposure are common, understanding the impact of heat on HF outcomes is critical.

Methods: Using 2021 Medicare data, we identified patients with HF in 11 western U.S. states. With a case-crossover design and conditional logistic regression, we estimated odds ratios for all-cause, CV, and HF-related hospitalizations and ER visits associated with extreme heat (daily maximum temperature above the 85th percentile of the local decadal average), adjusting for particulate matter (PM2.5) and public holidays, stratified by HF type.

Results: Among 43,447 HF patients (mean age 73 ± 16), extreme heat was associated with a 6% increase in all-cause hospitalization (OR 1.06; 95% CI 1.02–1.10), 10% increase in all-cause ER visits (OR 1.10; 95% CI 1.06–1.15), and 8% increase in CV-related ER visits (OR 1.09; 95% CI 1.02–1.16). Effects were seen primarily in HFpEF, not HFrEF; poor air quality was not significantly associated with outcomes.

Conclusion: Short-term extreme heat, but not poor air quality, was associated with increased hospitalization and ER visits among HF patients in the western U.S., especially those with HFpEF.

II.

Real-World Medication Safety and Effectiveness

I use large-scale real-world data to evaluate the safety and effectiveness of medications used in diabetes, cardiovascular disease, kidney disease, and aging populations. My work includes comparative effectiveness studies, treatment trajectory analyses, and medication safety evaluations using claims-based and linked datasets. This line of work includes studies on SGLT2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists, oral anticoagulants, exposure to nephrotoxic medications, treatment adherence, and cardiovascular and renal outcomes.

Related Publications ▾
Use of Potentially Nephrotoxic Drugs in Type 2 Diabetes Patients on SGLT2i: A Trajectories Analysis — Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf, 2025 DOI → ▾

Purpose: To characterize trajectories of nephrotoxic potential (NxP) drug use among older adults with type 2 diabetes (T2D) treated with SGLT2 inhibitors and identify associated patient characteristics.

Methods: Using 2012–2019 Medicare data, we selected patients with T2D who filled at least one SGLT2i prescription and quantified the number of NxP drugs used each month over the first 12 months. A group-based trajectory model identified groups with similar use patterns, and multinomial logistic regression examined associations between patient characteristics and group membership.

Results: Among 8,811 beneficiaries (mean age 67.5), we identified three trajectories: no (24%), low (54%), and high (22%) use of NxP drugs. Age, gender, low-income subsidy eligibility, and clinical characteristics were associated with group membership.

Conclusions: Three distinct trajectory groups emerged, with a substantial proportion showing low NxP drug use. Both social and clinical factors were associated with NxP drug use.

COVID-19 Diagnosis, Oral Anticoagulation, and Stroke Risk in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation — Am J Cardiovasc Drugs, 2024 DOI → ▾

Background: COVID-19 has been associated with increased stroke risk. It remained unclear whether this risk differed by oral anticoagulation (OAC) use among patients with atrial fibrillation.

Methods: Using insurance claims data from individuals with established atrial fibrillation, we used Cox proportional hazard models to assess relationships between anticoagulant use, COVID-19 diagnosis setting, and ischemic stroke.

Results: Among 561,758 participants (mean age 77), anticoagulation reduced stroke risk (HR 0.85), while COVID-19 elevated it substantially (HR 2.11), with the greatest elevation among hospitalized cases (HR 3.95). There was no significant interaction between OAC use and COVID-19 diagnosis (p = 0.96).

Conclusion: The increase in COVID-19–associated stroke risk was independent of whether patients received oral anticoagulation therapy.

Real-World Comparison of the Effectiveness and Safety of Apixaban, Dabigatran, Rivaroxaban, and Warfarin in Medicare Beneficiaries with Atrial Fibrillation — Am J Cardiol, 2020 DOI → ▾

It remained unknown whether the comparative effectiveness of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs) and warfarin differs between atrial fibrillation patients with and without a history of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA). Using 2012–2014 Medicare claims, we identified patients newly diagnosed with AF who initiated apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban, or warfarin, and constructed Cox proportional hazard models with treatment-by-history interaction terms.

DOACs were generally more effective than warfarin in stroke prevention, but with important subgroup differences. The superiority of dabigatran over warfarin was more pronounced in patients with a history of stroke or TIA (HR 0.64) than without (HR 0.94; p for interaction = 0.034). Among patients with prior stroke or TIA, risk was lower with dabigatran (HR 0.64) and rivaroxaban (HR 0.70) than with apixaban.

In conclusion, the comparative effectiveness of DOACs differs substantially by history of stroke or TIA; in particular, apixaban appeared less effective in patients with a prior stroke or TIA.

Identifying Patients at Risk of Acute Kidney Injury Among Medicare Beneficiaries with Type 2 Diabetes Initiating SGLT2 Inhibitors: A Machine Learning Approach — Front Pharmacol, 2022 DOI → ▾

Introduction: To predict acute kidney injury (AKI) risk in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) prescribed SGLT2 inhibitors.

Methods: Using a 5% random sample of Medicare claims, we identified 17,694 patients who filled SGLT2i prescriptions in 2013–2016, split equally into training and testing sets, and measured 65 candidate predictors from the prior year. We applied random forests (RF), elastic net, and LASSO for risk prediction.

Results: The AKI incidence rate was 1.1% over a median 1.5-year follow-up. RF produced the best prediction (C-statistic = 0.72). Among the top 10% by risk score, the actual AKI rate was 3.7%. Use of loop diuretics had the strongest association with AKI (adjusted OR 3.72).

Discussion: The machine learning model efficiently identified Medicare beneficiaries with T2D at risk of AKI during SGLT2i treatment.

Using Machine Learning to Identify Diabetes Patients with Canagliflozin Prescriptions at High Risk of Lower Extremity Amputation Using Real-World Data — Pharmacoepidemiol Drug Saf, 2021 DOI → ▾

Aims: Canagliflozin has been increasingly used in diabetes for its cardiovascular and renal benefits, but trials documented an increased risk of lower extremity amputation (LEA). We applied machine learning to predict LEA among diabetes patients treated with canagliflozin.

Methods: Using a 5% random sample of Medicare beneficiaries, we identified 13,904 patients initiating canagliflozin (April 2013–December 2016), split equally into training and testing sets, identified 41 candidate predictors, and applied elastic net, LASSO, gradient boosting, and random forests.

Results: The LEA incidence rate was 0.57% over a median 1.5-year follow-up. LASSO produced the best prediction (C-statistic 0.81). Among the top 5% by risk score, the actual LEA rate was 3.74%. History of LEA (adjusted OR 33.6) and loop diuretic use (adjusted OR 3.6) had the strongest associations.

Conclusions: The machine learning model efficiently predicted LEA risk and may support optimized treatment decisions for diabetes patients.

Individual and Social Determinants of Adherence to SGLT2 Inhibitor Therapy: A Trajectory Analysis — J Manag Care Spec Pharm, 2023 DOI → ▾

Objective: To identify distinct adherence trajectories to SGLT2 inhibitors among patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and examine patient characteristics and social determinants of health (SDOH) linked to adherence.

Methods: This retrospective cohort study used 2012–2016 Medicare claims to identify T2D patients initiating SGLT2i. Group-based trajectory models categorized adherence patterns, and multinomial logistic regression examined associations with patient characteristics.

Results: Among 6,719 patients, four trajectories emerged: continuously adherent (49.6%), early discontinuers (27.5%), late discontinuers (14.5%), and intermediately adherent (8.4%). Neighborhoods with higher unemployment, lower income, lower educational attainment, and worse health infrastructure showed reduced adherence.

Conclusions: Only half of patients remained continuously adherent during the first year, and several contextual SDOHs were associated with suboptimal adherence.
